Two Rims

Two people, Sam and Alex, having a conversation. You'll watch it, change one thing, and watch it again. Six short steps.

Each step asks you to guess before it shows you — a guess you've committed to is the only way to find out what you actually believed.

Sam and Alex are not real, and they are not you. This is a model — it has never met anyone, it measures nothing, and nothing you do here leaves your browser. It can't tell you about your relationship. It can show you a few things about how any two people's moods pull on each other.

    Three things to carry out

    • This was a model, not a measurement. Sam and Alex are a shape with dials. The shape is the published form of a real model of marriage; the numbers behind it are illustrative, not fitted to anyone.
    • The fortune-teller isn't a fraud. The famous "94%" and the replicated "29%" are honest answers to two different questions — how well you can explain a marriage you've already watched end, and how well you can call one that hasn't ended yet.
    • What every school of couples therapy agrees on isn't why couples hurt — it's what hurting looks like: one person pressing while the other pulls back. They argue about the cause. They describe the same thing.
