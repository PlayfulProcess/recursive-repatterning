# Do these traditions actually have a classification we can use as a "lens" over the NVC needs list?

Baseline: the CNVC Needs Inventory groups ~74 needs under headings including Connection, Physical Well-Being, Honesty, Play, Peace, Autonomy, and Meaning ([CNVC feelings-and-needs inventory](https://www.cnvc.org/store/feelings-and-needs-inventory); list reproduced at [Sociocracy For All](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/nvc-feelings-and-needs-list/)). The question: which other traditions offer a *published* classification (not one we'd have to invent) that could stand next to it as an alternate grouping of the same items.

## 1. Brené Brown

**What exists:** The Dare to Lead "Living Into Our Values" exercise is a single alphabetical list of ~100+ values (Accountability, Achievement, Adaptability… through the rest of the alphabet), published as a worksheet ([brenebrown.com/resources/dare-to-lead-list-of-values](https://brenebrown.com/resources/dare-to-lead-list-of-values/)). The instructions ask the reader to pick ~10, then narrow to two "umbrella" values, optionally by improvising their *own* clusters — there is no official grouping supplied ([Colin Breck's walkthrough of the exercise](https://blog.colinbreck.com/understanding-our-core-values-an-exercise-for-individuals-and-teams/); [PCE/USD summary](https://pce.sandiego.edu/dare-to-lead-values/)).

Separately, *Atlas of the Heart* (2021) organizes 87 emotions and experiences into 13 chapters, each framed as a destination — "Places We Go When Things Are Uncertain or Too Much," "…When We Search for Connection," "…When the Heart Is Open," "…When We Feel Wronged," "…to Self Assess," etc. ([Wikipedia: Atlas of the Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_of_the_Heart); chapter titles confirmed via [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/atlas-of-the-heart/summary/) and [15-Minute Business Books](https://www.15minutebusinessbooks.com/blog/2022/01/24/atlas-of-the-heart-by-brene-brown-here-are-my-six-lessons-and-takeaways/)). This is explicitly a map of *emotions and experiences*, not needs or values.

**Does it classify needs/values?** The values list: no grouping at all (deliberately ungrouped, user-clustered). Atlas of the Heart: classifies emotions, not needs.

**Verdict: not a lens as published.** Brown offers no existing taxonomy of needs or values we could borrow. Any "Atlas lens" over the NVC list (e.g., mapping Connection-needs to "Places We Go When We Search for Connection") would be *our* interpretive bridge from an emotion-map to a needs-map — legitimate as a design choice, but we'd have to own it as invented, not attributed to Brown as a needs classification.

**Suggested shape if built anyway:** a 13-chapter "signal map" — which need, when unmet, tends to surface as which Atlas-of-the-Heart emotion-family — framed explicitly as our synthesis.

## 2. EFT / attachment theory (two different "EFT"s — disambiguated)

**What exists:** Sue Johnson's *Emotionally Focused Therapy* (couples) puts attachment at the center and reduces it to a compact felt-security formula: **A.R.E.** — Accessible ("Do I matter?"), Responsive ("Can I depend on you?"), Engaged ("Do you cherish me?") — serving the two attachment functions of *safe haven* and *secure base* ([Maritial Intimacy Institute's A.R.E. assessment](https://maritalintimacyinst.com/wp-content/uploads/A-R-E-Assessment.pdf); [Heartfelt Counseling explainer](https://www.heartfeltcounselingmn.com/blog/2024/9/20/heres-what-we-all-want-in-our-relationships-are-a)). Those two functions trace to Bowlby's original four features of attachment — proximity maintenance, safe haven, secure base, separation distress — later made empirical by Ainsworth's Strange Situation studies ([Structural Learning summary of Bowlby](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/bowlbys-attachment-theory); [Simply Psychology on the secure-base concept](https://www.simplypsychology.com/articles/secure-base-attachment-concept)).

This is a *different* EFT from Leslie Greenberg's *Emotion-Focused Therapy*, an individual/intrapsychic approach the two co-originated in the 1980s before diverging — Johnson kept attachment and relational focus central, Greenberg moved toward experiential work on emotion schemes within the individual ([Psychotherapy Courses comparison](https://www.psychotherapycourses.com/what-is-eft-therapy-sue-johnsons-vs-leslie-greenbergs-approach/); [Anne Belgram Perkins' disambiguation](https://www.annebelgramperkins.com/differences-emotionally-focused-therapy-sue-johnson-emotion-focused-therapy-leslie-greenberg/)).

**Does it classify needs?** Yes, narrowly: attachment theory does propose a small, canonical set of relational needs (proximity, safe haven, secure base) and Johnson's A.R.E. is a genuine three-part classification of what "being there" means relationally.

**Verdict: honest lens, but narrow.** It's real and citable, but it only covers relational/Connection-type needs — it isn't built to classify Physical Well-Being, Play, Meaning, etc. Using it as a lens over the *whole* needs list would require stretching it (interpretive), but using it over the Connection cluster specifically is faithful to the source.

**Suggested shape:** a 3-way sub-lens (Accessible / Responsive / Engaged) applied only to Connection-family needs, explicitly scoped as "how attachment science reads the Connection needs," not a whole-inventory lens.

## 3. Lisa Feldman Barrett / constructionism

**What exists:** The theory of constructed emotion holds that emotion categories are constructed, situated concepts rather than fixed biological fingerprints; the raw ingredients are *core affect* (valence × arousal) plus interoception, and concepts get recruited to predict and regulate the body's energy budget (*allostasis*) ([Wikipedia: Theory of constructed emotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_constructed_emotion); [Barrett et al., "The Theory of Constructed Emotion: More Than a Feeling," 2025, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/17456916251319045)).

**Does it classify needs/values?** No. It's a process theory of how *any* category (including "need" words) gets built in context — it offers no fixed list of needs, values, or even emotions.

**Verdict: not a lens; a design constraint.** There is nothing to map onto — building a "constructionist lens" would mean designing our own dimension (e.g., situating each need by the valence/arousal state and social context it's typically invoked in), which is entirely our invention wearing Barrett's vocabulary.

## 4. Bonus — taxonomies that genuinely exist

- **Max-Neef's Fundamental Human Needs**: 9 needs (Subsistence, Protection, Affection, Understanding, Participation, Idleness/Leisure, Creation, Identity, Freedom) crossed with 4 existential categories (Being, Having, Doing, Interacting) — a real 9×4 matrix, non-hierarchical ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Max-Neef%27s_Fundamental_human_needs)).
- **Maslow's hierarchy**: 5 tiers — physiological, safety, love/belonging, esteem, self-actualization ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslow's_hierarchy_of_needs); [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/maslow.html)).

Both are published needs classifications and map onto the NVC list with only modest interpretation (they were built to classify needs, not something adjacent).

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Max-Neef** — build first: a real 9×4 needs matrix, least interpretive stretch.
2. **Maslow** — build second: familiar, genuinely a needs hierarchy, easy onboarding lens.
3. **Attachment/A.R.E.** — build third, scoped only to Connection-family needs; label it as partial coverage.
4. **Atlas of the Heart** — build only as an explicitly-labeled "our synthesis" signal-map, not presented as Brown's own needs taxonomy.
5. **Constructionist lens** — skip, or build as a reflective/meta layer ("notice the situation, not the label") rather than a needs-grouping lens.
