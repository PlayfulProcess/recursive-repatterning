# Research: affect as a signal toward needs — the overflow hypothesis

*Working document. This is the long-form register: hypotheses, caveats, half-settled
claims, and the record of what was checked. Public-facing copy states findings cleanly;
this file shows the work. Companion changes landed with this doc: the
`schools/ericksonian-hypnosis/` grammar, the `trance-and-suggestion` branch, and the
Overflow deck's new guide card, The Fit.*

---

## 1. The hypothesis, stated carefully

The candidate key hypothesis for this library:

> **Affective states are usable as signals about needs being met or unmet. NVC's
> needs vocabulary is an unusually good concept-set to hand the predicting brain.
> Needs and wants can be distinguished by their fruits — by whether satisfying them
> produces overflow or requires the next extraction. Held this way, the barrier
> between serving life in oneself and serving it in others loses its load-bearing
> role, and guilt gives way to responsibility: the error goes on my heap because
> only I can compost it.**

Every clause of that is defensible. But the joints have to be honest, and the
load-bearing joint is weaker than the hypothesis wants it to be.

### 1a. What Barrett's research actually supports — and the correction that improves it

Barrett's constructed-emotion account supports: affect is real, low-dimensional
(valence and arousal), continuous, and reads the state of the body budget. It does
**not** establish that affect is a signal of *specific needs*. "This tightness means
my need for autonomy is unmet" is a *concept applied to affect* — an interpretation.
That interpretive move is precisely NVC's move, and NVC is a practice, not a finding.

The maintainer's correction to an earlier draft of this argument, adopted here
because it is both more accurate to Barrett and stronger for the hypothesis:
**affect is not a message waiting to be read — it is the readout of a body already
preparing to act.** Allostasis is anticipatory: the brain budgets for action before
the action, and before the name. By the time a feeling is nameable, the body has
already leaned in a behavioral direction. Interpretation does not *decode* the
signal; it arrives after the mobilization and can, with effort, *interrupt or
redirect* it. And the fight with the body's inbuilt patterns is a real fight —
prediction runs first, deep, and cheap, and deliberate override runs late, shallow,
and expensive. This is why repatterning is slow, why insight alone is weak, and why
state-first traditions (see §4) keep independently rediscovering the same lever.

So the corrected hypothesis reads: *affect is the felt readout of anticipatory
action; needs-vocabulary is a chosen concept-set for steering that action once it is
felt; and the choice among concept-sets is judged by fruits.* Held as "Barrett proves
NVC," the hypothesis is the overreach this library exists to refuse. Held as "NVC is
a well-aimed map laid over a real substrate," it is fully consistent with the creed —
and it is arguably the library's implicit thesis made explicit: the substrate says
maps are constructed; this says how to choose among them.

### 1b. Interoception, defined

**Interoception** is the nervous system's sensing of the body's internal state — the
continuous afferent traffic from heart, lungs, gut, vessels, immune system, and
hormonal milieu, integrated (on Barrett's account) into the low-dimensional summary
we experience as core affect: feeling good or bad, worked-up or becalmed. It is to
the inside of the body what vision is to the world — and, like vision, it is not a
passive readout: the brain *predicts* interoceptive input and corrects against it,
which is why expectation, framing, and concept can literally change how the body
feels from the inside. In this library's terms: interoception is the gauge; the
budget is what it gauges; concepts are the dial-markings we paint on it.

### 1c. Needs vs. wants, operationally

Essentialist need-lists invite taxonomy wars. The overflow criterion sidesteps them:

- A **need**, satisfied, produces fullness that spills — the satisfaction ends the
  seeking and frees energy outward (overflow).
- A **want**, satisfied, requires the next acquisition — the satisfaction sharpens
  the seeking (extraction, run inward).

This is a fruits-test, not a category-test: the same object can be need or want in
different hands or different seasons. It is also deliberately compatible with the
six-needs framework's own best observation (see §4): its "needs of the personality"
can be met by strategies that relieve without fulfilling — which is the same cut,
made from the other side.

### 1d. Guilt → responsibility; the self/other barrier

Guilt, in NVC's reading, is a *should* turned inward — moral arithmetic pointed at
the self (the Ledger). Responsibility is the overflow-compatible replacement: *only
I can act here, so the error goes on my heap* (the Error, the Heap). Nothing new
needs building; the deck already carries both cards.

The self/other claim needs one guard rail. As an **energy** claim it is the creed:
overflow does not check the beneficiary's ID; serving life in myself and in others
is one motion. As a **boundary** claim it can slide into self-erasure — serving
others *from* emptiness, which is sacrifice, which the Pour card already refuses.
The Two Truths card is the standing guard: participation and individuation, both,
and the part that says no survives the dissolution.

## 2. Context is the first clinical fact (the population argument)

The sharpest claim to come out of this inquiry, now a guide card (The Fit):
**treatment is contextual to the population it was built to serve.**

- A state-first method presupposes a client with slack — someone whose material
  conditions permit "state is a choice" to be approximately true.
- Aimed at someone whose affect is accurately tracking precarity, the same method
  does harm twice: it misreads a structural signal as a personal state-management
  failure, and it trains the person to override the one honest gauge they have.
- This is Barrett-consistent, not just politically flavored: affect reads the
  *actual* budget, and some budgets are drained by systems, not by unhelpful
  predictions. Individual state-work on a structurally drained budget treats the
  gauge instead of the leak.

Andreotti's *Hospicing Modernity* names the wider pattern: modernity dresses its
historically specific values — linear progress, compulsory expansion — in the
borrowed authority of nature. "Grow or die," offered as a law of life, is that move
in miniature: living systems also mature, rest, hold steady, and senesce; a cell
that grows without limit is called cancer, not health. The library's rendering of
this critique lives in the ericksonian-hypnosis grammar's `human-needs-coaching`
Research note (growth-as-law, population fit, evidence), where the repo's own voice
is allowed to disagree with a school it holds.

The affirmative version of the same point: a person contributing to their community
inside a traditional life is meeting connection and contribution about as directly
as humans have ever met them. It is the *ordering* of the six-needs hierarchy —
growth as law, significance as engine — that is the modernity layer, not the needs
themselves. What the maintainer is reaching for is close to a collective wise mind
(Linehan's term, held in schools/dbt-skills): neither raw emotion-mind nor bloodless
reasonable-mind, but affect deliberately re-wired, through chosen concepts, toward a
vision of collective overflow.

## 3. The "unconscious mind" as a clinically effective fiction

Erickson's warm unconscious — a storehouse of learnings, not Freud's cellar — is not
sustainable as anatomy on a predictive-processing account: one predicting brain, not
two minds. But the maintainer's instinct here is right and worth recording: **Barrett
would likely grant the construct's clinical utility while denying its ontology,**
and her repeated public point cuts the same way — people assume *less*
responsibility for change than they actually have, because prediction feels like
perception and cultivating different predictions feels impossible until practiced.
"Trust your unconscious" works, when it works, as a well-aimed reframe: it reassigns
competence to the patient, lowers effortful over-control, and licenses change
without demanding insight first. The library's standard applies: a made meaning,
adopted for its effects, held visibly as made. (Rendered in the school's
`the-unconscious-as-resource` Research note.)

The same discipline, run in the other direction, marks the lineage's evidence
gradient — recorded in the school's root: Ericksonian hypnotherapy proper, *growing*
(a 2025 meta-analysis of eight RCTs: large effects vs. inactive controls, parity
with CBT/MI; small base); SFBT, the lineage's best-evidenced branch; NLP, repeatedly
failed controlled tests; six-needs coaching, no independent trials. Methods and
warrant travel separately; the loudest branch is not the best-evidenced one.

## 4. Candidate card: The Interrupt (drafted, not dealt)

The Fit went into the deck as a second Guide card — it is meta to the deck (how to
choose maps at all), so it sits beside The Spread without disturbing the 21.

A second candidate came out of §1a and is drafted here but **not** added, because it
would be a 22nd numbered card: the deck's spine is 3×7, The Spread says "shuffle the
21 numbered cards," and breaking that symmetry is the maintainer's call, not a
research doc's. Options if wanted: add it and re-speak the Spread (3×7+1, the odd
card as the spiral's hinge); or fold its content into The Prediction's sections;
or swap it for a card the deck could lose (none obviously volunteers).

> **The Interrupt** — Process.
>
> *Body:* The feeling arrives already moving. By the time it has a name, the body
> has leaned — budget allocated, direction chosen, an action already priced. Affect
> is not a message waiting for a reader; it is the readout of a departure in
> progress. The interrupt is the narrow, expensive, real capacity to catch the
> lean before it becomes the step. It is not repression — repression denies the
> readout. It is not obedience — obedience calls the lean a verdict. It is a hand
> laid gently on a moving thing, and it costs every time, and the cost is the
> practice. The fight with the body's inbuilt patterns is a real fight. Honor it
> as one: train it, budget for it, and do not shame the gauge for the direction
> of the lean.
>
> *In Process:* interrupting now — the hand on the moving thing, this breath.
> *In Structure:* the interrupts you've automated; which leans no longer even reach
> the step, and what that vigilance costs the budget.
> *In Possibility:* a lean that no longer needs interrupting — the pattern
> re-trained at the source, the expensive override made unnecessary by a cheaper
> prediction. Repatterning is the interrupt retiring itself.

## 5. Structural notes (answers the maintainer asked for)

- **Can branches hold practitioners?** The chassis pattern is: branch → schools
  (grammars) → items. A practitioner goes in as an *item* inside the school whose
  tradition they work — as human-needs-coaching now sits inside
  ericksonian-hypnosis. A hypothetical Tillich school could likewise carry a
  Parker-Palmer-lineage item at pointer depth. When a practitioner's own body of
  work grows past pointer depth, the one-source rule promotes it to its own
  grammar, cross-linked with the standard pill — never stacked into the parent's
  items.
- **Grammar with sources and caveats?** Both, per the existing conventions:
  faithful presentation in the school's own sections; the library's disagreements
  (evidence limits, growth-as-law, population fit) in Research notes and this doc;
  `evidence_tier`/`evidence_note` doing the differentiation an undifferentiated
  badge would fake.
