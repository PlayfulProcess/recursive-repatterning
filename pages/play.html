Draw from the schools

Draw a reading from one school of emotion, or across several at once, and hold what comes up as a mirror, never a diagnosis. This is play in Tolkien's sense: you're a sub-creator, a maker of meaning you step into with your judgment intact — never a verdict you obey.

One Casting a reading — a gate, not a diagnosis

A drawn reading isn't a clinical assessment — this whole project is built on saying so plainly, and we make no claim that a drawn card foretells anything or names what you "really" feel. What we hold open, instead, is a quieter hypothesis: that something in the practice of drawing and reflecting may yet be worth keeping, whatever school's vocabulary you draw it through. We don't adjudicate between the ways people find it useful — only keep the door open:

  • The empirically-minded may value the move that art and narrative therapy are built on — externalising: put the difficulty out there, on a card, and you can turn it over in your hands. You can do that by simply journaling, by building your own school on recursive.eco, or by making one directly with Claude Code.
  • The researcher, weary for an afternoon of the discipline of never accepting what cannot be observed, can sit with a drawn feeling and stay open to the hypotheses we will never have evidence for — much as Carlo Rovelli, in Helgoland, wonders what the fire he is watching really is.
  • The Jungian may find an archetypal dialogue with the figures the draw surfaces.
  • The lover of stories may hold the draw the way Tolkien held myth — not as a lie but as sub-creation: a made thing that can tell a true thing slant. To pretend, deliberately and with both eyes open — to make-believe — is one of the oldest ways our species has made meaning, and it lifts rather than deceives.
  • And when a reading lands uncannily, the quieter machinery is worth naming without explaining the meaning away — conceptual blending, the Barnum effect, cold reading, depth psychology, and narrative therapy. Meaning made is still meaning.

Two Help keep it alive

This is a public commons — every school a plain JSON grammar, every claim carrying its own evidence tier, the whole library forkable. The loop only closes when the people who use the tool also tend it: what the anthropologist Christopher Kelty calls a recursive public — a community that maintains the very means of its own coming-together. That is the wager of recursive.eco, in miniature.
